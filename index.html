Occultations

“Occultations” describe an astronomical event where a celestial body closer blocks the view of another more distant—completely obscuring it from an observers perspective. Hiding it. When do occultations become more than a celestial event? Welcome to the website. A literary log of literary censorship.

—Henrich Heine, Alamansor, 1821

What this site does Every entry below is a real or reconstructed record of a text that was suppressed — with the who, when, and why restored where possible.
A history of erasure

Timeline

399 BCE

The Execution of Socrates, Athens

After being brought to court by Meletus, Anytus, and Lycon, Socrates was found guilty of impiety and corrupting Athenian youth. He died after drinking a cup of Poison Hemlock and liquid.

213 BCE

The Qin Dynasty Book Burning

Under Emperor Qin Shi Huang, the burning of books, or 焚书 (Fenshu), occurred to eliminate all political dissent and unify the state’s ideology under Legalism, excessive conformity to a single law.

48 BCE - 391 CE

The Burning of the Library of Alexandria

The slow destruction and political neglect of the ancient institution that once gave rise to scholars such as Eratosthenes and Archimedes.

1849

The Mock Execution of Fyodr Dostoevsky

Dostoevsky’s execution by a firing squad was a form of psychological torture for members of the philosophical Petrashevsky Circle, who were too progressive for Tsar Nicholas I.

1933

Nazi book burnings

Student groups across Germany burn tens of thousands of volumes deemed "un-German," including works by Freud, Mann, and Hemingway.

1878

Vernacular Press Act

Colonial Law enacted by Viceroy Lord Lytton during the British Raj to suppress criticism of British policies in regional and native Indian languages, while imposing no such regulations on English-language publications. Known as the “Gagging Act”.

2020s

A new wave of school book bans

U.S. school districts see a sharp rise in formal challenges and removals, concentrated on race, gender, and sexuality.

Case Studies

CASE NO. 001 Executed

The Execution of Socrates, Philosopher of Athens and Father of Modern Philosophy

399 BCE ·

Accused by Meletus, Anytus, and Lycon for impiety and corrupting the youth, Socrates refused escaping trial, and was executed after drinking poison Hemlock.

CASE NO. 002 Rescued

Mock Execution of Fyodor Dostoevsky, One of the Finest Novelists to live

December 22,1849

The mock execution was a form of psychological torture in which members of the Petrashevsky Circle were arranged against a firing squad only to recieve a last-minute pardon from the tsar.

CASE NO. 003 Vanished

The Burning of the Library of Alexandria

Destroyed over many years.

At first, burned during the Egyptian Civil War by Julius Caesar. Later, during attempts to wipe out paganism by Emperor Theodosius, and also by increasing neglect towards knowledge over time.

CASE NO. 004 Abolished

The Vernacular Press Act of India

Salman Rushdie · 1988 · Fatwa issued 1989

Banned outright in several countries; translators and publishers faced violence for years after the fatwa was issued, reshaping global debate on speech and blasphemy.

Why this archive exists

About

Censorship rarely announces itself. It instead arrives as a quiet edit, a quick reconsideration, an “open discussion”, or a “revised for content” sticker no one reads twice. Eventually, when great works of literature and art are seized from us, it rarely feels like a blessing; instead, it leads us into an era of confusion, disillusionment, and stagnation.

The Occultations project aims to track these moments of erasure and keep a record of what was removed. The log isn’t here to persuade you, but it can provide you with a distinct narrative on what happens when these moments of seizure arrive, and what they mean to us, as humanity.

Entries here draw on trial records, publisher correspondence, banned-book lists, and reporting, cited in full below. Corrections and additions are welcome..
